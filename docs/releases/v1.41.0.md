Arabic as Office in Arabic works with it: Hijri dates in Worksheets and Calendar, numbers in Arabic's own digits, and Find that reads a word however it is written.

## Every app

- **Find passes over the marks a word is written with**, as Office in
  Arabic does by default: كتب finds كَتَبَ and كتـــب, احمد finds أحمد, and علي
  finds على. The vowel marks (harakat), the kashida and the forms of alef
  and of yeh are set aside, and so are Urdu's and Persian's own yeh and
  kaf. A match takes its marks along when it is replaced. An Arabic or
  Urdu window's Find in Documents and Presentations has a Match diacritics
  box to find a word only as it is written. Worksheets' Find does the same.

## Worksheets

- **Hijri dates**: a number format can name the Hijri calendar as Excel's
  do, with B2 before it or the calendar in its locale code
  (`[$-60401]d mmmm yyyy` gives 29 ربيع الآخر 1448). B1 is Gregorian. The
  dates follow the Umm al-Qura calendar, so a file Excel dates by its
  older Kuwaiti reckoning can differ from it by a day.
- **Digits in a number format**: `[$-2000000]` writes a number in
  Arabic-Indic digits (١,٢٣٤.٥٠) and `[$-3000000]` in Urdu's (۱۲۳), as Excel
  reads and writes them.
- An Arabic or Urdu window's number format list offers Hijri date, Hijri
  long date and a number in the language's own digits.

## Calendar

- **The Hijri calendar beside the Gregorian**, as Office's second calendar:
  each day's Hijri number in the month, week and day views, a month's name
  on its first day, and the Hijri months in the title. On by default in an
  Arabic or Urdu window, off elsewhere; the Hijri button turns it either
  way and the choice is kept.

## Checked

- Engine tests hold Find to Arabic's marks and letter forms in a worksheet,
  a document and a deck, and number formats to the Hijri calendar and both
  sets of digits.
- The Arabic and Urdu window checks open Calendar too: the Hijri day beside
  each day, a Hijri month named in its own script, the Hijri button pressed.
