PDF part 2: comments and markup, forms and Fill & Sign, redaction that takes the words out of the file, bookmarks kept, pictures and smaller files; no Soon button left; panels you can resize in every app; and a visual pass over all of them.

## PDF

- **Comment and mark up.** The Comment tab highlights, underlines and
  strikes through the words you select, places sticky notes and text
  boxes, and draws freehand, rectangles, ellipses, lines and arrows, in
  six colours. Each comment is written into the file as Acrobat writes
  it, with its own appearance, so other readers show it as this window
  does. The Comments pane lists every comment by page: answer one, mark
  it resolved, change its words or delete it with its replies. Comments
  other people made are read, listed and kept.
- **Forms and Fill & Sign.** Form fields fill in place (text, check
  boxes, radio buttons, lists), each value written with the appearance
  other readers show. Fill & Sign places your signature or initials,
  typed, drawn on a pad, or from a photo of one signed on paper with the
  paper made see-through, and today's date, a checkmark or words. It says
  plainly that these are pictures, not digital signatures.
- **Redaction that removes.** Mark Text, Mark Area and Mark Matches mark
  what is to go; Apply Redactions takes the words and the pictures'
  pixels out of the pages themselves, and the same words out of
  everywhere else in the file: the document's properties and metadata,
  bookmarks, comments and their replies, links, attached files, form
  values and page labels. The dialog lists what else it found, by kind,
  and any picture it had to leave off, by page. The result is a new file
  written whole, because an update would keep the old bytes.
- **Bookmarks, page labels, links and form fields** now survive Save,
  Extract, Split and Merge Files, with a Bookmarks pane and each page's
  label shown.
- **Export as Pictures** (PNG or JPEG, 72 to 600 dots an inch) and
  **Reduce File Size**, which redraws pictures at the size the page
  needs and saves a smaller copy.
- Not yet: words in a text box or form value in Urdu, Arabic or Hindi
  are kept in the file but drawn by the reader rather than given an
  appearance of their own; lines and shapes under a redaction mark are
  covered, not cut away; Reduce File Size leaves CMYK and masked pictures
  alone.

## Every app

- **No Soon button is left.** Mark All as Read in Presentations' comments
  and Design, Slide Size (Standard, Widescreen or a size of your own,
  with Maximize or Ensure Fit) are built. The buttons that only work with
  an online service (Translate, Dictate, Forms, Presenter Coach, Online
  Videos, Automate a Task, From Web, Keep Slides Updated, live subtitles)
  are gone rather than greyed, and so are Macros and Ink to Math; a
  file's macros are still kept untouched.
- **Panels you can resize.** The panels beside the work show a grip on
  their inner edge: drag it, move it with the arrow keys, or double-click
  to reset, and each app remembers the width.
- **Galleries shrink before they fold.** As the window narrows, Word's
  Styles and Style Set and Presentations' Themes and Variants lose one
  choice at a time, with up, down and more arrows, before their group
  folds into a button; at 1300 px Documents keeps its Styles open.
- **A visual pass** over every app at two window sizes, light and dark:
  Mail's key hint, the launcher filling a tall window, Image's tool rail
  on a short one, Contacts' labels and dark-theme links, and ribbon
  details put right.

## Video

- The preview and the timeline share the window by a divider that drags,
  moves with the arrow keys, resets on a double-click and is remembered;
  at 1300 by 700 the preview has more than half the height, where it had
  about a sixth. The Scenes panel resizes, and the timeline's track names
  and each lane's height drag and are kept.

## Calendar

- The month's seven columns are one width whatever the events in them,
  and a full day ends in "+N more"; the week and day views line up their
  columns, open on the working morning, and show short events' titles.
