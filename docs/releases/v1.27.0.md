Password-protected Word, Excel and PowerPoint files open and save, in all
three document apps, which also gain an accessibility checker and a spelling
pass with a dictionary of your own. In Rutba Word: text boxes, drawings put

## Password-protected files, in Word, Worksheets and Presentation

A `.docx`, `.xlsx` or `.pptx` that Office protected with a password now
opens. The window asks for the password in a small dialog that names the
file. A wrong password is refused in place so you can try again. A file
changed since its password was set is refused, with a sentence saying so,
because the check written inside it no longer matches. Both kinds of
encryption Office has written are read: Agile, from Office 2010 on, and
Standard, from Office 2007. File → Info → Protect Document (Protect
Workbook, Protect Presentation) → Encrypt with Password sets one, and every
save after that is encrypted as Office 2013 and later do it: AES-256,
SHA-512, 100,000 rounds, in the compound file Word, Excel and PowerPoint
expect. Clearing the password saves the file plainly again. The password is
kept only in memory for the open document. The autosave copy of a protected
document is encrypted with the same password, so what is left on disk after
a crash is never the document in the clear.

## Review, in Word, Worksheets and Presentation

Review → Check Accessibility opens an Accessibility pane that sorts what
makes a file hard to use with a screen reader or low vision into Errors,
Warnings and Tips, under the rule names Office uses: missing alternative
text, a table without a header row, a slide with no title or the same title
as another, text too pale for its background, headings that skip a level,
merged cells, link text that says nothing, a slide's reading order, empty
paragraphs used for spacing, sheets still called Sheet1, and a file with no
title. Each finding takes you to its picture, cell, paragraph or slide, says
why it matters and how to fix it, and most have a one-click fix. With "Keep
accessibility checker running" ticked, the status bar reads "Good to go" or
"Investigate" as you edit. Alt Text, on the Review tab and the right-click
menu, writes the description where Word, Excel and PowerPoint keep it, and
Mark as decorative writes Office's own mark.

Review → Spelling (F7) checks the whole file from the caret in an Editor
pane. It shows the word in its sentence with suggestions and offers Change,
Change All, Ignore Once, Ignore All and Add to Dictionary, then "Spelling
check complete". It reads a document's body, tables, text boxes, notes,
headers and footers; every text cell of every sheet; and every slide's
shapes, tables and notes. Two dictionaries come with the suite, English
(United Kingdom) and English (United States), and a check never goes to the
network. The language is the document's own, or else the system's. A
personal dictionary shared by the three apps is kept in the Custom
Dictionary dialog, which edits, imports and exports it. In Word a right-click
on a misspelt word offers its suggestions. A dictionary is read on its own
thread the first time it is needed, so a window never waits for one.

## Rutba Word

- **Insert → Text Box.** Simple Text Box, a sidebar, a pull quote, a WordArt
  box, or a box drawn by dragging on the page. It is written the way Word
  2010 and later write one, and you type in it with the usual formatting. It
  moves by its edge and resizes by eight handles, as Word's does. A Shape
  Format tab sets its fill, outline, inner margins, text alignment and text
  direction, and its height and width.
- **Layout → Hyphenation.** None, Automatic, Manual and Hyphenation
  Options. Manual walks the words a hyphen would pull back and puts an
  optional hyphen where you press Yes. The options set the zone, a limit on
  hyphens in a row, and whether words in capitals may be broken. They are
  saved in the file as Word saves them, and a paragraph can be left whole.
  The page and the printout break words at the same places. The suite
  carries its own English hyphenator, because Chromium on Windows has none.
- **References → Citations & Bibliography.** Manage Sources keeps a master
  list in your profile and the document's own list inside the file, where
  Word keeps it. Create Source asks for the fields Word asks for a book, a
  book section, a journal article, conference proceedings, a report or a
  web site. Insert Citation writes a citation Word itself can update, and
  Add New Placeholder cites a source you will fill in later. The Style list
  gives APA (7th and 6th), MLA, Chicago, Harvard and IEEE, and changing it
  redraws every citation at once. Bibliography inserts a Bibliography,
  References or Works Cited list. Update Citations and Bibliography, or F9,
  rewrites both.
- **Review → Restrict Editing.** Word's pane: formatting limited to
  styles, and editing limited to No changes, Tracked changes, Comments or
  Filling in forms. Paragraphs can be marked as exceptions anyone may edit.
  Yes, Start Enforcing Protection takes an optional password and writes it
  as Word does, and the window then keeps to it. Locked text refuses a
  keystroke with Word's own sentence. The regions anyone may edit are
  washed yellow between brackets, with Find Next Region I Can Edit.
  Protection for tracked changes keeps recording on. Stop Protection asks
  for the password. A document Word protected opens protected, with the
  pane open.
- **Layout → Arrange for every drawing.** Behind Text and In Front of Text
  draw a picture or a box under or over the words, on screen and on paper.
  Bring Forward, Send Backward, Bring to Front and Send to Back set the
  drawing order. Align lines drawings up to the margin, the page or each
  other, and Distribute spaces them evenly. Rotate and Flip turn them, and
  Group and Ungroup join them into one. A Selection Pane beside the page
  lists them, picks them, hides and shows them, and renames them.
  Navigation and Selection panes now sit beside the page, not under it.
- **Insert → Caption.** A dialog offers Figure, Table or Equation and a
  line of text, previews "Figure 3: …" before OK is pressed, and inserts a
  new paragraph after the caret's — styled "Caption" (written once, italic,
  9pt, space after, the way Word writes one on a document that never had
  the style), the label, a SEQ field for its running number, then the
  words. The number is a COMPLEX field — `w:fldChar` begin/separate/end
  around ` SEQ Figure \* ARABIC `, the shape Word itself writes for a
  caption, unlike a cross-reference's simpler `w:fldSimple` — shaded on the
  page exactly as a REF field is. Update Fields (F9, on the References tab)
  now renumbers every label's captions from where they sit in the document,
  the same pass that refreshes a REF: a caption inserted ahead of others
  with the same label, or one deleted, leaves the rest right once Update
  Fields runs. The button was greyed out ("A caption is a SEQ field; fields
  are not written yet").

- **Mailings → mail merge.** The Mailings tab works. Start Mail Merge makes
  a document a letter, e-mail messages, envelopes, labels or a directory,
  kept in the file as Word keeps it, so a merge letter opens again attached
  to its list and on the record it was showing. Select Recipients reads a
  workbook's sheet, a .csv or .tsv, the suite's own Contacts, or a list
  typed into a new address list. Edit Recipient List ticks people in and
  out, sorts, filters and finds duplicates. Address Block (blank lines left
  out, the home country dropped), Greeting Line ("Dear Sir or Madam," when
  the name is missing), Insert Merge Field and Rules (If…Then…Else, Next
  Record, Skip Record If and more) write Word's own fields; Highlight
  Merge Fields shades them. Preview Results shows any record's words in
  their place, with a record box and Find Recipient. Finish & Merge makes
  Letters1 with a letter per record, prints them, or sends one e-mail per
  record through Mail from the account you choose, with progress and any
  failures named.

- **Mailings → Envelopes and Labels.** Envelopes takes the delivery address
  from the selection or the address book and a remembered return address,
  on DL, C4, C5, C6, B5, No. 10, Monarch and other sizes, previewed to
  scale; Add to Document puts the envelope in front of the letter as a page
  of its own size. Labels prints one address on a full sheet or a single
  label, on fourteen common Avery sheets, and New Document builds the sheet
  as Word does; a labels merge fills each label with the next record. Along
  the way print and PDF learnt two things they lacked: each section prints
  on its own paper (a landscape section used to print portrait), and a
  line break inside a paragraph ends the line on paper (it used to be
  joined up).

- **Equations, read, drawn, written.** A document's equations used to be
  missing from the page altogether — Word's own quadratic formula simply
  was not there. They now open drawn as Word draws them, in Cambria Math: a
  real fraction bar, a radical over its expression, limits stacked above
  and below a sum, brackets that grow, matrices, accents, italic variables
  and upright function names. Each is one character to the caret: the
  arrows step over it, a click selects it, Backspace takes it and Undo
  brings the same one back, and a save writes it back exactly as it came.
  Insert → Equation (or Alt+=) opens an editor that takes Word's linear
  format — `x=(-b±√(b^2-4ac))/2a`, `\sum_(i=1)^n i`, `\alpha` — with a live
  preview, Word's Structures (Fraction, Script, Radical, Integral, Large
  Operator, Bracket, Function, Matrix), a Symbols palette and the built-in
  equations (the area of a circle, the binomial theorem, the Pythagorean
  theorem, a Fourier series, the quadratic formula and more); it writes the
  OMML Word writes. A double-click on an equation opens it again. Printing
  and PDF draw each equation as the page shows it. Not yet: bold, font and
  size applied inside an equation, and equations in a deck.

- **References → Table of Contents, a field that refreshes.** Until now the
  button typed the headings in as text, which went stale the moment a
  heading changed. It now writes the table Word writes: a building block
  holding a TOC field, one paragraph per heading at three levels, each
  entry a link to a bookmark placed on its heading, a dotted leader and
  the page number the heading lands on in this window's own page layout.
  Update Table, beside it, rebuilds the entries from the headings as they
  now stand (and F9 refreshes it with the other fields); Ctrl+click on an
  entry goes to its heading; Remove Table of Contents takes it away. A
  table written by Word itself is read as the same field and refreshes the
  same way.

- **Review → Track Changes records.** Turning it on is kept in the file, so
  a document opens recording if it was saved that way. While it is on,
  words typed are kept as an insertion and words deleted as a deletion,
  each with its author and time, rather than simply appearing or going;
  deleting words you typed yourself since turning it on takes them straight
  back, as Word does. The Review tab shows it four ways: All Markup
  (insertions underlined and deletions struck through in the author's
  colour), Simple Markup (the finished words with a bar in the margin
  beside each changed paragraph — the default, as in Word), No Markup and
  Original. Accept and Reject act on the change at the caret or on every
  change; Previous and Next walk them. What is not recorded yet: a
  paragraph joined or split (Enter and Backspace across a paragraph's end
  happen for real), a paste, find and replace, and a sort. The buttons
  were greyed out ("recording them is not built yet").

## Worksheets

- **Insert → PivotChart.** A chart bound to the PivotTable the cursor is in,
  or a PivotChart & PivotTable made together from the list around it. It is
  written as Excel writes one and redrawn whenever the pivot is refreshed or
  filtered.
- **Page Layout → Arrange.** A sheet's pictures, shapes, charts and
  slicers can be picked (Ctrl+click adds), dragged, resized by eight
  handles, turned, and nudged with the arrow keys. Bring Forward, Send
  Backward, Bring to Front and Send to Back set the order. Align,
  Distribute, Rotate and Flip line them up and turn them. Group and Ungroup
  join them into one, and a Selection Pane lists them front first, to pick,
  hide, show and rename. Every drawing keeps its anchor to the cells as
  Excel keeps it.
- **Insert → Slicer.** A panel of buttons for each field of a table or a
  pivot. Press one item, Ctrl+click or turn on Multi-Select for several, or
  Clear Filter. The table's rows or the pivot's items follow at once. A
  slicer is dragged and resized like any drawing, written as Excel 2010 and
  later write slicers, and read back from Excel's own files. The PivotTable
  dialog now picks its fields from the list.
- **Formulas → Watch Window.** The button now opens a pane docked along the
  bottom of the grid — a table, one row per watched cell: Sheet, Cell,
  Value and Formula. Add Watch adds every cell of the current selection,
  not only the active one; a row's own × takes it off, and Delete Watch
  removes whichever row is selected; a double-click on a row goes to that
  cell, switching sheet first when it is on another one. Every row is
  live: read straight off the calculation on each frame, the same way
  Error Checking rereads its list, so editing a precedent anywhere in the
  workbook moves the watched value at once, with nothing telling the pane
  to refresh. The list is kept for as long as the workbook stays open —
  closing the pane or switching sheets never loses it — but it is never
  written to the file: Excel does not keep its own watch window in the
  workbook either, and this one follows suit. The button was greyed out.

- **Review → Protect Workbook and Allow Edit Ranges.** Protect Workbook
  locks the workbook's structure, with an optional password hashed the way
  Excel hashes one (a file an older Excel locked with its short password
  still opens with it). While it is on, no sheet is added, deleted,
  renamed, moved, hidden or shown again — each refused in Excel's words —
  and the tab menu, the + and a padlock by the tabs say so. The tab menu
  now also hides, unhides and moves sheets. Allow Edit Ranges lists the
  ranges a protected sheet leaves open, each with a title, its cells and an
  optional password; typing into one asks for its password once, then the
  key you typed goes into the cell.

- **View → Page Layout, Custom Views, and a sheet background.** Page Layout
  shows the sheet on the pages it prints on, cut exactly where the printer
  cuts it, with margins, rulers, the header and footer where they print
  ("Click to add header" writes one) and blank pages past the data to type
  onto. Custom Views keeps how a workbook looks — zoom, selection, hidden
  rows and columns, filter, print settings — under a name and shows it
  again, written as Excel writes them. Page Layout → Background tiles a
  picture behind the cells, left off the printout as Excel leaves it.

- **Data → Consolidate and Forecast Sheet.** Consolidate sums, averages,
  counts or finds the largest of ranges from several sheets, by position or
  by the labels in their top row and left column; with links to the source
  it writes formulas over detail rows folded under an outline, as Excel
  does, and remembers what it last consolidated. Forecast Sheet previews,
  then makes a new sheet in front of the data with Excel's forecast table —
  FORECAST.ETS past the last point and the confidence bounds either side —
  and a chart that shades the band between them. FORECAST.ETS,
  FORECAST.ETS.CONFINT, FORECAST.ETS.SEASONALITY and FORECAST.LINEAR now
  calculate, by additive Holt-Winters smoothing; Microsoft does not publish
  how Excel picks its smoothing constants, so the figures are sound but
  will not always match Excel's to the last digit.

- **Formulas → Evaluate Formula.** The active cell's formula with the part
  it will work out next underlined; Evaluate puts that part's value in its
  place, ranges shown as Excel shows arrays (`{5;3}`), IF, IFERROR and
  CHOOSE stepping only the branch they take. Step In opens the formula of
  the cell the underlined reference points at; Step Out brings its value
  back; at the end the button reads Restart.

- **Formulas → Calculation Options.** Automatic, Automatic except for data
  tables, or Manual, kept in the workbook. In Manual what depends on an
  edit waits until F9 (Calculate Now, every sheet) or Shift+F9 (Calculate
  Sheet), and the status bar says Calculate meanwhile — press it to
  calculate. Saving calculates first, as Excel does.

- **Review → Comments, as conversations.** New Comment (Ctrl+Alt+M) opens
  a card beside the cell signed with your name and the time, with replies,
  Resolve and Reopen, Edit and Delete; the cell carries Excel's purple
  corner, grey once resolved. Previous and Next walk the threads across
  sheets, and Show Comments lists them in a pane, open or resolved. They
  are written as Excel 365 writes threaded comments, with the plain copy
  older versions show, and Excel's own open as conversations — before, only
  their plain copy showed.

- **Insert → Scatter.** X against Y: markers only, straight lines or smooth
  lines, drawn with a numeric X axis and written as Excel's scatter chart.

- **View → Page Break Preview and Split.** The sheet as the printer will
  cut it: what is not printed greyed, pages edged in blue and numbered,
  breaks the paper makes dashed and breaks put by hand solid; drag a break
  and it stays where it is dropped. Normal and Page Break Preview buttons
  sit beside the zoom. Split divides the window at the active cell into
  panes that scroll on their own, with bars to move the split. A 60,000-row
  sheet still draws a frame in about 20 ms in either.

- **Data → Group, Ungroup and the outline.** Rows or columns grouped
  (Shift+Alt+Right) get the gutter Excel draws: a bracket in their level's
  lane, a − box at the summary row that folds them away and turns to +,
  and level buttons 1, 2, 3 in the corner. Groups nest seven deep; Show
  Detail, Hide Detail, Ungroup (Shift+Alt+Left) and Clear Outline act as
  Excel's do, and a selection that is neither whole rows nor whole columns
  is asked about. The file keeps what Excel keeps — each row's and
  column's level, hidden and collapsed marks, the sheet's depth — so an
  outline saved by Excel opens folded or open as it was left. Frozen panes
  and the gutter live together.

- **Data → Subtotal.** At each change in a column, a row of SUBTOTAL
  formulas under every group for the columns ticked, a bold "East Total"
  label, a Grand Total and a three-level outline round them; all eleven of
  Excel's functions, page breaks between groups, summaries above or below,
  and Remove All to take it away. A SUBTOTAL now leaves out the SUBTOTAL
  cells in its own range, as Excel's does, so a Grand Total counts each row
  once. On a 60,000-row list Subtotal takes a few seconds and a frame still
  draws in about 6 ms.

- **Data → Advanced, and Clear.** A list filtered by a criteria range read
  the way Excel reads one: headings name the fields, conditions on a row
  all hold, any one row is enough; `>`, `<=`, `<>` compare, `*`, `?` and `~`
  are wildcards, and a formula under a heading of its own is tried on every
  row. In place, rows that fail are hidden and Clear brings them back;
  copied, the rows that pass go elsewhere under the headings, with Unique
  records only dropping repeats. The names Excel keeps for the list, the
  criteria and the extract are written too.

- **Data → Flash Fill (Ctrl+E).** Type what you want once or twice beside a
  block — "Ada" next to "Lovelace, Ada", or "AL" for initials — and the
  rest of the column is filled. It finds one transformation that makes
  every example: a part cut at a delimiter, a word, digits, the first or
  last characters, initials, a change of case, part of a date, or pieces of
  several columns joined with words of your own. When nothing fits every
  example it says so and fills nothing.

## Presentation

- **Home → Find (Ctrl+F) and Replace (Ctrl+H).** The ribbon's Find and
  Replace buttons now open a small pane at the stage's own top right, the
  way Word's find pane sits over the page rather than a dialog covering
  the slide. A word typed into it is found across every shape's paragraphs
  on every slide — text boxes, placeholders and table cells — read as "n
  of m", Next and Previous walking the hits in slide order and landing on
  the hit's own slide and shape each time; Match case narrows the search.
  Replace rewrites the current hit, Replace All every hit at once, each
  keeping its run's own look — bold, italic, colour, font, size — since
  only the words inside the run change. A match that would straddle two
  runs is not offered: the boundary between two runs is where their look
  can change, and rewriting across it would either lose the second run's
  formatting or have to invent a blend of the two, so only a match sitting
  inside a single run is one Replace can rewrite cleanly. The button was
  greyed out (`docs/GAPS.md`: "find and replace across a deck").

- **Design → Themes, Variants, Colours, Fonts, Effects.** Eleven themes of
  our own — Rutba, Harbour, Meadow, Ember, Nightfall, Paper Kite, Basalt,
  Saffron, Tidewater, Orchard and Studio — each with four variants, light
  and dark. The gallery shows this slide drawn in each one by the deck's own
  renderer, not a stock picture. A pick rewrites the theme and the master's
  background, and every slide follows while keeping what it states itself.
  Colours offers our palettes and Customise Colours (the twelve slots, with
  a sample); Fonts offers heading and body pairs and Customise Fonts;
  Effects sets how theme-styled shapes are filled, outlined and lifted.
  Text now takes its size, colour and typeface from the master and the
  theme as PowerPoint draws it — a title in a deck made here is 44 pt, as
  its master says, where it used to come out at 32.

- **Undo and Redo in Presentation.** Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y, one step per edit.
  Presentation had no undo at all until now.

- **View → Slide Master.** The master and its layouts on the stage, with
  PowerPoint's prompts in dashed frames and the number of slides on each
  layout. Formatting the master's title or body sets the text style every
  slide inherits; shapes and backgrounds on the master or a layout sit
  under every slide on it. Insert Layout, Rename, Delete (an unused
  layout), Preserve, Title and Footers, Insert Placeholder and Hide
  Background Graphics work as in PowerPoint, and Close Master View goes
  back to the slides, redrawn. The handout and notes masters are not
  editable yet.

- **Review → Comments.** New Comment on a shape or on the slide, replies,
  Resolve, Delete, Previous and Next across the deck, and a Comments pane,
  with markers on the slide and in the strip; written as PowerPoint 365
  writes its modern comments and signed with your name. Comments from older
  PowerPoint are read and can be deleted.

- **Equations on a slide.** Equations PowerPoint wrote are drawn — a sum
  with its limits, a real fraction bar — on the stage, in the strip, in the
  show and on paper, and saved back exactly as they came; before, they did
  not show at all. Insert → Equation opens the same editor as Rutba Word
  and writes Office Math with a picture beside it for older readers, as
  PowerPoint does; a double-click opens one again.

- **Transitions.** The Transitions tab is live: None, Cut, Fade, Push,
  Wipe, Split, Uncover, Cover, Random Bars, Shape and Dissolve, each with a
  small picture of its own. A pick previews itself on the stage and Preview
  plays it again; Effect Options sets the direction, Duration the length,
  On Mouse Click and After how the slide moves on, and Apply To All puts it
  on every slide. A slide with a transition gets PowerPoint's star under its
  number in the strip. The show plays each one as it moves forward and
  moves on by itself when After says so. The file carries it as PowerPoint
  writes it, and a PowerPoint 2010 effect already in a deck (Prism,
  Gallery) is kept and plays as the fallback PowerPoint stored beside it.

- **Animations.** Select a shape and pick Appear, Fade, Fly In, Float In,
  Split, Wipe or Zoom; More Effects and Add Animation add Pulse, Spin and
  Grow/Shrink and the exits. Effect Options, Start (On Click, With
  Previous, After Previous), Duration and Delay set each one, and Move
  Earlier and Later reorder them. The Animation Pane lists the slide's
  effects with their click numbers, how each starts and a star in its
  colour; rows are dragged, retimed from a right-click, removed or all
  played. Numbered tags sit beside each animated shape while the tab is
  open, and the Animation Painter copies one shape's effects onto another.
  In the show, shapes that enter start hidden and come in a click at a
  time; a click during an effect finishes it, and the click after the last
  one leaves the slide; the presenter view's Next plays the next effect
  too. Files keep PowerPoint's own timing tree and preset numbers, and
  what this does not write — motion paths, media, triggers — survives
  untouched. Not yet: an effect played by paragraph (the whole shape
  animates), a transition's sound and an animation's trigger.

- **Home → Arrange: align, distribute, rotate, flip, group.** Shift+click
  or Ctrl+click gathers shapes into one selection, drawn as one frame;
  dragging any of them moves them all, and Delete and the arrow keys act on
  the lot. Align puts one shape against the slide's edge or centre, and
  several against the edge of the group they make (or of the slide, with
  Align to Slide ticked); Distribute spaces three or more evenly. Rotate
  Right and Left turn by a quarter, Flip Vertical and Horizontal mirror, a
  handle above a single shape turns it by hand (Shift for 15° steps), and
  Alt+Left and Alt+Right turn it by 15° from the keyboard. Group makes one
  shape of several, which moves, resizes, copies and deletes as one and
  sits as one row in the Selection pane; Ungroup puts every member back
  exactly where it now shows, however the group was moved, stretched,
  turned or flipped in between. A group that came in a file now draws its
  members stretched and flipped with it — before, they were only moved.

- **Shape Fill and Shape Effects.** The Format pane's fill is No fill,
  Solid (now with transparency), Gradient (a light or a dark variation of a
  colour, at any angle) or Picture (a picture from a file, filling the
  shape's outline, stretched or tiled). Shape Effects keeps its shadows and
  gains Glow (5, 8, 11 and 18 pt, PowerPoint's own sizes), Soft Edges and
  Reflection (tight, half, full), in any combination, drawn on the slide,
  in the show and in print, and written the way PowerPoint writes them.

## Mail

- **A signature per account.** Set from that account's own settings —
  Folder → Signature — a signature is plain text, kept on the account
  alongside its name and its servers rather than in a preference every
  account shares. A new message carries it at the end, after a blank
  line and the "-- " line mail readers use to tell a signature from the
  words above it; a reply or forward puts the same block above the
  quoted or forwarded message instead, below the cursor rather than
  under it. Switching the From account in an open message swaps the
  block for the one that account keeps — but only when it is still
  exactly what was inserted; a signature already edited, or a body with
  nothing inserted to begin with, is left alone. An account with no
  signature has nothing added.

- **Send later, and an Outbox that is a real place.** Send in a new
  message now offers Send later: later today, tomorrow morning at 8, Monday
  morning at 8, or a date and time of your own, all in the computer's own
  time zone. The message waits in an Outbox kept on this computer, listed
  in the folder list with each message's send time; opening one offers
  Edit (back into the message window), Send now, or Cancel, which puts it
  in Drafts rather than throwing it away. It is sent when its time comes
  while Rutba Office is running, and anything that fell due while it was
  closed goes the moment it opens again; the window says so. A send that
  fails stays in the Outbox with the server's answer beside it and is tried
  again every five minutes, or at once with Retry, where before it was
  moved to Drafts after three tries without a word.

- **Automatic replies, per account.** Folder → Out of office turns them on
  for the account, with an optional start and end, a subject ("Automatic
  reply: " and the original subject unless you write your own), the
  message, and "only reply to people in my contacts". A banner across the
  window says which account is answering on its own, with Turn off beside
  it. A reply keeps the rules that stop two away messages mailing each
  other forever (RFC 3834): nothing already automatic, no bulk or list
  mail, no no-reply or mailer-daemon address and never the account's own
  address gets one, and each sender hears from it once while it is on. The
  reply says what it is (`Auto-Submitted: auto-replied`) and threads under
  the message it answers. Replies go out while Rutba Office is running:
  holding them on the server (Gmail's vacation responder, Microsoft 365's
  automatic replies) needs a sign-in permission this version does not ask
