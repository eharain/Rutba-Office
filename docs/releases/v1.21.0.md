A cell's words move in, and turn.

## Worksheets

- **Home → Increase indent, Decrease indent.** The two arrows move a cell's
  words in and out by one of Excel's indent units a press, each cell from
  its own indent, and never below none. Written as Excel keeps an indent —
  in the cell's alignment, beside a left alignment when the cell had none.
  Centring a cell drops its indent, as in Excel. The buttons were greyed
  out; an indent in a file was drawn and none could be given.

- **Home → Text orientation.** Angle anticlockwise, Angle clockwise,
  Vertical text, Rotate text up, Rotate text down, and Horizontal text to
  put it back: the words turn on screen and on the printed page, the row
  growing to hold them, and the file carries Excel's own rotation so Excel
  shows the same. The button was greyed out before.

## Proven, not promised

- Ten engine tests set an indent and read it beside the left alignment,
  step two cells from their own indents and stop at zero, keep a right
  alignment's indent and drop a centred cell's, write each of the five
  orientations and read them back after a save, take a rotation off, and
  undo both with the style table.
- Three window checks press Increase indent twice on A2 and read the words
  move in by nine pixels a press, pick Rotate text up and read the words
  standing on end with the row grown, save, and read the indent and the
  rotation back from the file (`RUTBA_VERIFY_ONLY=links`).

## Under the hood

- `setFormat({ indent, indentBy, rotation })` in `applyFormat` beside
  align, valign and wrap; `formatOf` and the frame's cells carry `indent`
  and `rotation`; the grid and the print page turn the words alone, not
  the cell.
