Pictures and shapes on a slide, drawings on a sheet, and the suite keeps itself current.

1.5.0 gave Presentation PowerPoint's ribbon and left two of its buttons
honest and grey: Pictures, and Shapes. Both are live now. And a second
company's files — a profile deck, a product document, a pricing sheet, a
delivery diagram — were opened beside Microsoft's own rendering of each, and
what differed was fixed, engine by engine.

## Automatic updates, on

An installed copy now checks GitHub's release feed on its own: twenty-five
seconds after launch and every six hours, one request carrying nothing about
the person or the machine. A newer release downloads in the background and
installs the next time the suite is quit; nothing is installed while anyone
is working. **About → Check for updates automatically** turns it off, and off
means no request at all. A suite that ships a release a day and never says so
leaves every installed copy on the version it came with, defects and all;
this is the fix for that.

## Pictures on a slide

- **Insert → Pictures** puts a picture from this device onto the current
  slide: PNG, JPEG, GIF or BMP. The bytes go into the package as a media
  part, the slide gets a relationship to it, and the slide gets a `p:pic`
  whose blip names the relationship — the three things PowerPoint insists
  on — with the extension's content type declared once, as Office declares
  it, not as an override per picture.
- **Sized from its own header.** The service reads the width and height
  out of the first bytes, as the photo viewer does for a folder of two
  thousand, and fits the picture to the slide keeping its aspect. A small
  picture keeps its own size; a tiny one gets a hand's width to resize from.
- Selected as soon as it lands, in the thumbnail and the sorter at once.

## Shapes on a slide

- **Shapes**, on Home and on Insert, is a menu of fifteen preset geometries
  in PowerPoint's own names — Rectangle, Rectangle: Rounded Corners, Oval,
  Isosceles Triangle, Right Triangle, Diamond, Parallelogram, Trapezoid,
  Pentagon, Hexagon, Octagon, Star: 5 Points, Arrow: Right, Chevron, Line —
  each drawn as its geometry.
- Written the way PowerPoint writes a shape it has just drawn: preset
  geometry, a solid fill in the theme's first accent, a line in the same
  accent darkened by half, a text body anchored to the middle. A theme
  change recolours it. It lands in the middle of the slide, selected; the
  Font and Paragraph groups act on its text, Arrange nudges and deletes it.

## Drawings on a sheet

A diagram drawn in Excel — boxes, arrows, braces, connectors — opened as an
empty grid with its captions. The engine had read the drawing part since
charts were built; the window never painted it.

- Every shape, picture and chart on a sheet is now drawn over the cells,
  in the cells' own coordinates, at the size its author drew. (A connector
  one pixel wide and a column tall was being widened to eighty and drawn as
  a diagonal.)
- **Connectors** are read — they were "unknown" — and run corner to corner
  of their box, flipped as the box says; a bent connector turns half way.
- **Braces and brackets** are drawn as strokes with no body.
- A shape that states no fill and no line of its own takes them from the
  theme through its style references, as Excel does: an arrow Excel filled
  in the first accent was transparent here. A scheme colour with a shade
  child no longer reads the shade's number as the colour.
- A rotated shape turns about its centre; a brace laid on its side under a
  box is drawn on its side.

## Worksheets

- `CEILING.MATH` and `FLOOR.MATH`, and the `_xlfn.` prefix Excel writes in
  front of any function newer than 2007 is the file's, not the function's:
  a pricing sheet that showed `#NAME?` in four cells shows its numbers.

## Presentation, measured against PowerPoint

- **Letter spacing** on a run is drawn; a site address set wide is wide.
- **Faces with no lowercase** — Bebas Neue is on every second deck — set
  their text in capitals, and fall back to a condensed face on a machine
  without them. The capitals are the design.
- **Bullets stored for Wingdings or Symbol** are drawn as the character
  they look like: "§" in Wingdings is a small square, and was a "§".
- A wrapped line is matched back onto its source word by word, so a line
  with a double space in it keeps its last character: "7LP  UK" ended "U",
  and "Info@zeptosystems.com" ended "Info@ze".

## Word, measured against Word

- A run that says **colour auto** is black, whatever colour its style says
  — a pasted heading was blue here and black in Word.
- **Contextual spacing** ("don't add space between paragraphs of the same
  style") is honoured: a list sits tight while it keeps its space from the
  prose around it.
- A list's marker **hangs** to the left of the text by the level's hanging
  indent: the bullet at a quarter inch, the text at a half, as Word draws
  it. And the bullet is the character the numbering stored, mapped through
  the same symbol-font table as the slides.

## The checks

- Nine engine tests: a picture round-trips with its bytes, its part, a
  default content type and no override; a shape round-trips with the theme
  accent, its line, centred text and its geometry; CEILING.MATH the 2013
  way; a connector's flips, a brace's path, a style reference's fill, a
  rotation; colour auto, contextual spacing and the hanging marker on a
  page; the symbol-font bullets.
- Two application checks: a picture sent through the document service
  lands on the slide, drawn and in its thumbnail; Home → Shapes → Oval puts
  an oval on the slide, filled, selected, drawn as an ellipse.
- The harness itself: its windows open on a second display when there is
  one, because twenty windows on the display somebody is working at get
  closed by hand, and closing them takes the checks with them; the border
  check accepts any border, since Chromium floors a 1px border to 0.571px
  on a 175% display; the mail block waits for its rows instead of pausing
  a fixed time that was enough only on an idle machine; the Bold check
  clicks a shape that has text.
