A paragraph of pictures splits between its picture lines, and pictures in a paragraph of their own sit side by side.

## Rutba Word

- **A paragraph of pictures no longer runs over the page's edge.** A
  scanner writes its scans into one paragraph — four cards, two to a line —
  and a paragraph was split only at a line a character begins; a line of
  pictures has none, so the paragraph stayed whole and its second line was
  drawn across the gap between the sheets (owner, 2026-09-20, after 1.17.0:
  "it still is the same"). The pictures under a paragraph's words now
  continue its address space — the text's length, then a picture's index —
  so a split can land between two picture lines, the part on the next page
  drawing the pictures from that line on. The rule that never leaves one
  line alone on the next page gives way when the paragraph heads its page
  or is taller than one, where the alternative was running over the edge.
- **Pictures in a paragraph of their own sit side by side**, as Word draws
  inline pictures: as many to a line as fit, placed by the paragraph's
  alignment. Under words a picture is still a block beneath them. The empty
  line that stood above a paragraph's pictures is gone.
- **Print places the pictures a page at a time**: the ones that fit go
  down on the page and the rest head the next, each still whole.

## Worksheets

- **A character typed briskly into a cell is never lost.** The first key
  starts the edit and the editor is drawn a frame before it takes focus; a
  character arriving in that frame went to the grid, which had no use for
  it. It is text now, like the ones that arrive while the edit is still
  beginning.

## Proven, not promised

- One engine test prints a paragraph of four pictures two to a page, none
  scaled, none cut.
- Two window checks open a paragraph of six scans of a card, read two to
  a line, the first two under the heading and the other four on the next
  page, none crossing a sheet's edge, and put the caret into the second
  part without an error (`RUTBA_VERIFY_ONLY=cards`). The real-input check
  that types "4321" into a cell at 60 ms a key is the one that caught the
  lost character.

## Under the hood

- A part carries `data-length` and `data-tail`, each picture `data-image`;
  `pages.js` splits and pulls back through `offsetAtLine`; the caret in a
  part that holds only pictures goes to the end of the words.
- `paginate.js` places `images` fragments in batches that fit the page.
