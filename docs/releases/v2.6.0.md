Rutba Office 2.6.0 lets you choose which of the eleven apps you want. Recorder takes still pictures and records a part of a screen, and Mail writes a message in the whole window when you want the room.

## Choose your apps

- **Choose apps.** The launcher has a Choose apps button beside its list of
  apps. Turn an app off and its tile, its templates and its place in the
  template gallery go. It stays installed, so turning it on again is one
  click, with no download.
- **Asked once.** On first start a card says all the apps are on, and offers
  to keep them all or choose fewer. It does not come back.
- **In the installer too.** The Windows installer has a Choose apps page after
  the folder. Office takes that choice on its first start. A newer installer
  shows the boxes as you last left them, and an update keeps your choice.
- **Your files go where you want them.** On Windows, an app turned off stops
  opening its kinds of file. Turn off Worksheets, for example, and .xlsx files
  go back to whatever opened them before. Turned on again, Office takes back
  only the kinds of file nothing else has taken since.

## Recorder

- **Region.** Choose Region, pick a screen or a window, and drag over the
  picture to record only that part of it. Drag again for a smaller part, or
  choose Whole picture to record all of it.
- **Take Picture.** Keep a still picture as a PNG beside your recordings. Take
  what is shown, with the camera corner, the title and the logo. Or take the
  screen, the window or the region at its own size, or the camera alone. Take
  it at once or after 3, 5 or 10 seconds, with a countdown on the picture.
  Then open it, edit it in Image, copy it or show it in its folder. It works
  while you record or stream too.

## Mail

- **Full view for writing.** A new message, a reply, a forward or a draft can
  fill the whole window instead of the box over the mailbox. Choose Full view,
  or Smaller to go back. What you wrote, the formatting and the attachments
  stay, and the next message opens the way you left it.

## Also

- **Passwords on Linux.** A Linux desktop with no keyring now stores no
  passwords or keys at all, as a computer with no keystore already did,
  instead of keeping them under a fixed key. This is ready for the Linux
  builds; the published downloads are still Windows only.
