Review tools in Documents and Presentations (forms, redaction, Inspect Document, ink, and Compare with tracked changes on a deck), Mail indexing on threads of its own with archives past 2 GB, signed and encrypted mail, and accounts added several at once.

## Documents

- **Forms, as Word makes them.** A Developer tab puts rich text, plain
  text, check box, combo box, drop-down list, date and picture controls
  in a document, with Properties (title, tag, placeholder, list items,
  date format, locked) and Design Mode. Word's older form fields are read
  and filled in place, and Restrict Editing's "Filling in forms" lets only
  the fields take input, Tab moving between them.
- **Redact.** Review, Redact marks the selection, every match of a Find,
  or patterns (email addresses, telephone numbers, card numbers, a pattern
  of your own) and areas of pictures. Apply saves a copy in which the
  marked words are gone from everywhere in the file: the body, headers and
  footers, notes, comments, tracked changes, field results, alt text and
  the document's properties, with black bars of the same length in their
  place.
- **Inspect Document** (File, Info, Check for Issues) finds and, when you
  ask, removes comments, tracked changes, hidden text, personal
  information, custom XML, headers and footers, invisible content and the
  document's properties, as Word's Document Inspector does.
- **Ink on the page** is saved as Word saves it, and ink drawn in Word
  opens. Highlighter strokes, which drew invisible in every app because a
  colour's transparency was read as the colour, now show.

## Presentations

- **Compare, and tracked changes on a deck.** Review, Compare merges a
  reviewed copy: a Revisions pane lists what changed slide by slide and
  shape by shape (words in and out, a shape added, removed, moved,
  resized or restyled, a slide added, removed or moved), each marked on
  the slide, and every change is accepted or rejected one at a time, by
  slide or all at once. The saved deck holds what was accepted.

## Mail

- **Indexing on threads of its own.** Archives and accounts are indexed
  and searched away from the windows, so a large .pst no longer holds the
  suite while it is read. A search answers from what is indexed so far
  and says how far it has got, and indexing can be stopped and resumed.
- **Archives past 2 GB.** An mbox or Outlook for Mac archive of any size
  opens where it lies, read a piece at a time.
- **Signed and encrypted mail (S/MIME).** A signed message says who
  signed it and whether the message is as they sent it, checked on this
  computer against the certificates Windows trusts; revocation is not
  checked, and the message says so. A message signed by someone other
  than its sender, or with no sender, or whose certificate has since
  expired, is not shown as good. An encrypted message opens with a
  certificate and key you import from a .p12 or .pfx file, the key kept
  in the system's protected store. Compose can sign and encrypt.
- **Accounts, several at once.** Add account takes rows of addresses and
  passwords pasted or loaded from a file (separated by spaces, tabs,
  commas, semicolons, colons or bars, or a table with headings), sets
  each up and reports how each went. It says how an account signs in (in
  a browser, with an app password, or with a password) and offers the
  browsers installed on this computer for signing in.
- **What it looks up, and when.** Settings are looked up only when you
  press Add, from a table of the large providers and then the address's
  own domain's records, and never from anyone else's service. A password
  goes to a server outside the address's domain only after you agree,
  and never without encryption.

## Text recognition

- Reading a page as a document is in the engine: it tells an invoice, a
  receipt, a letter and a form apart, finds a bill's supplier, number,
  dates, subtotal, tax, total and lines of items, checks that the sums
  agree, and reads a form's tick boxes. The windows will offer it next.
  The boxes drawn over recognised words keep their own height on a page
  that was turned straight, and beside a large heading.

## Checked

- A security review of the S/MIME and account code before release. Its
  findings are fixed: reading a signed or encrypted message runs on a
  thread of its own with size, structure and time limits, so no message
  can hold the suite or exhaust its memory; certificates are kept only
  from good signatures for the sender's own address and never swapped
  without asking; chains are checked for mail; weak algorithms are
  marked; Encrypt with Bcc is refused; a decrypted message that is not
  validly signed is shown as plain text; and the account setup's lookups
  are confined as above.
- Engine tests for forms, redaction (every redacted word searched for in
  every part of the saved package), Inspect, ink, deck comparison, the
  S/MIME reader and writer (interoperating with OpenSSL both ways), the
  archive index threads, the large-archive reader, the account rows and
  setup, and document reading.
- Window checks `word-forms`, `word-redact`, `inspect`, `word-ink`,
  `deck-compare`, `mail-index-worker`, `mail-big-mbox`, `smime` and
  `mail-accounts`.
- The fuzzer damaged 5,400 files at seed 5013: 2,529 opened and 2,871
  refused in a sentence, none faulted. The PDF reader's fuzzer damaged
  1,800 PDFs at seed 5014: 1,583 read and 217 refused in a sentence, none
  faulted, none hung. A first run of it on a heavily loaded machine
  timed some files out at its two-second limit; each of those files, run
  alone through everything the fuzzer does, took under 60 ms. The S/MIME
  reader was fuzzed separately with 2,000 damaged messages, none faulted,
  the slowest 47 ms.
