A tenth app for PDF pages, a templates gallery and QR codes in the three editors, and Mail as a tool for archives: search everywhere at once and export for legal hold.

## PDF, the tenth app

- **A tenth app for reading PDFs and putting their pages in order.** Open
  a PDF and its pages appear beside a strip of thumbnails, read one at a
  time, two side by side or in one column, at the window's width, the
  whole page or any zoom with Ctrl and the wheel. The words on each page
  can be selected and copied, and Find searches every page and marks what
  it finds.
- **Pages**: picked with a click, Ctrl and Shift, and dragged into a new
  order; rotated, deleted, extracted to a new file, and inserted from
  another PDF. A PDF splits into files of so many pages or at the pages
  picked, and several files merge into one. Undo and redo cover every
  change.
- **Saving never redraws a page**: each page goes into the new file exactly
  as it was, with its fonts, pictures and text. Save asks before it
  replaces the file the pages came from, and offers a copy instead. Unsaved
  work is kept for the launcher's Recover.
- Double-clicking a PDF now opens it in PDF; Pictures offers Open in PDF
  for one it shows; the launcher's PDF card combines files into one. The
  window speaks Urdu, Arabic and Hindi, right to left in Urdu and Arabic,
  the pages keeping their own direction. Pages are drawn by pdf.js, which
  ships with the suite and works with the network off.
- Not yet (part 2): forms and signatures, annotation, redaction, editing
  text in place, export to Word, OCR; bookmarks, page labels and form
  fields are not carried through a reorder, merge or split; CJK text in a
  font the PDF does not embed does not draw.

## Documents, Worksheets and Presentations

- **A templates gallery**, as Word, Excel and PowerPoint open with one: six
  documents (a letter, a CV, a report with a cover page and contents, an
  invoice, meeting minutes, a newsletter), six workbooks with working
  formulas (a monthly budget with charts, an invoice that adds itself up, a
  timesheet, a loan calculator with a yearly schedule, a to-do list, an
  accounts ledger) and four decks (a pitch deck, a project status report, a
  photo album, a training deck). Each card is a picture the suite drew of
  its template. Start something, More templates on the launcher shows the
  whole gallery by app, category and search; New from Template in each
  app's menu shows that app's, with a blank one first; templates you saved
  yourself are under Personal. Choosing one opens an untitled copy. The
  launcher's Letter, Report, Budget, Invoice and Pitch deck now start from
  these. The gallery speaks Urdu, Arabic and Hindi; the words inside the
  templates are English for now.
- **Insert, QR Code** turns a web address or any words into a code a phone
  can scan, with the error correction, size and colour chosen and a preview
  as you type. The code goes in as a picture whose alt text is its words,
  so a screen reader reads them too, and a double-click changes it in
  place. The codes come from the suite's own encoder, every version from 1
  to 40.

## Mail

- **Archives open where they lie**: an Outlook data file (.pst or .ost),
  an Outlook for Mac archive (.olm) or an mbox opens without importing,
  listed under Archives, read like a mailbox and never written to; a strip
  above its list says so.
- **Search everywhere at once**: the search box searches the open folder
  or All mail, every account and every open archive together, message
  bodies included, and each result says which mailbox or archive and which
  folder it lives in. It knows the syntax people already use: from:, to:,
  cc:, subject:, filename:, has:attachment, is:unread, after:, before:,
  received: with ranges, older_than:, size>, larger:, in:, a minus to
  leave something out, OR, and quoted phrases. A new Search tab offers the
  same through Scope and Refine, and Search syntax lists them all.
- **Export for legal hold** writes a search's results, a folder or several
  folders to an mbox and to PDFs (one per message, or one file after a
  summary page) with the attachments optionally beside them, and a
  manifest in CSV and JSON giving each message's date, sender, recipients,
  subject, Message-ID, source, folder and the SHA-256 of what was written.
  It shows progress, can be cancelled, and the sources are only ever read.
- Fixed: attachment names written in the encoded form some mail programs
  use for non-English names (RFC 2231) are decoded.

## Checked

- Engine tests: the PDF reader and page writer against files made by the
  suite's writer, its incremental updater and by hand (object streams,
  compressed cross-references, a hybrid file, an update that redefines a
  page, a damaged table, a protected file, links to pages kept and
  deleted); every template exactly what its specification makes through
  the engines today, its figures recalculating; the QR encoder against the
  standard's worked example, its format table and an independent
  encoder module for module, and every version and level read back; every
  search operator across a .pst, an .olm and an account; an export read
  back with every hash matched and the sources left byte for byte.
- A 5 GB Outlook data file of 100,000 messages, written at test time,
  opens in a quarter of a second, is indexed with its bodies in about 25
  seconds and answers searches in 19 to 210 ms; the 1 GB version is held
  to budgets in the performance pass.
- Window checks: `pdf-app` (22) reorders, rotates, deletes, inserts,
  saves, splits and merges and reads every file back; `templates` (13)
  opens copies of three templates and lists a template of one's own;
  `qrcode` (9) decodes each app's QR from the saved file; `mail-archive`
  (17) searches two archives and an account together and checks the
  export's mbox, PDFs and hashes, in Urdu and Arabic too. The paint check
  now photographs the PDF window as well.
- The fuzzer damaged 5,400 files at seed 4613, new documents, workbooks and
  decks and every old-format file among the fixtures: 2,528 opened and
  2,872 refused in a sentence, none faulted. A fuzzer of its own for the
  PDF reader damaged 900 PDFs at seed 4614: 797 read and 103 refused in a
  sentence, none faulted, none hung.
