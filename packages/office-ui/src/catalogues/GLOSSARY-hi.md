# Hindi glossary for Rutba Office

The words the Hindi catalogue (`hi.js`) uses for the suite's recurring terms,
so the same thing is called the same everywhere. It follows the words people
already know from Microsoft Office and Windows in Hindi, in standard Hindi
written in Devanagari, and plain everyday Hindi where Office has no word.

**This translation was drafted by machine and needs a native speaker's
review** before it is called finished. A reviewer changes a word here first,
then in `hi.js`, so the two stay in step.

## How the catalogue is written

- **Commands** take the polite imperative, as Office in Hindi does:
  सहेजें (Save), खोलें (Open), बंद करें (Close), सम्मिलित करें (Insert).
- **Names of things** (a tab, a group, a pane) take the noun: होम, डिज़ाइन,
  लेआउट, समीक्षा, दृश्य.
- **Sentences** (tooltips, messages, questions) are full, clear standard
  Hindi, addressing the reader politely (आप).
- **Placeholders** such as `{name}` and `{count}` stay exactly as written, in
  braces, untranslated; Hindi word order moves them where Hindi wants them.
- **Keys and shortcuts** stay as they are: Ctrl+S, F7, Shift+F2.
- **File extensions, formats and codes** stay as they are: .docx, PDF, CSV,
  `=SUM(ABOVE)`, `dd/mm/yyyy`.
- **Brand and product names** stay in Latin letters: Rutba Office, Word,
  Excel, PowerPoint, Office, Windows, Google, Microsoft, iCloud, SmartArt,
  WordArt, PivotTable, Power Query.
- **Punctuation**: a sentence ends with the danda । ; the comma and question
  mark are as in English; the ellipsis … stays at the end of a command that
  opens a dialog, as in English.
- **Counts** use Hindi's two plural forms, `one` (for 0 and 1) and `other`:
  `{ one: '{count} स्लाइड', other: '{count} स्लाइड' }`, the noun in its
  plural where Hindi changes it (`{count} पंक्ति` / `{count} पंक्तियाँ`).
- **Numbers** are written with the digits 0 to 9 in the catalogue; the window
  shows them grouped as India groups them (12,34,567), in Western or
  Devanagari digits by the digits setting.

## Errors and plain sentences

Error messages and tooltips are full sentences: translate the meaning, in
clear standard Hindi, not word for word. Keep a sentence's facts (a number,
a name in `{braces}`, a key) exactly. Prefer "यह फ़ाइल नहीं खुल सकी।" over a
literal rendering.
