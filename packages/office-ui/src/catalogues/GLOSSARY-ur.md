# Urdu glossary for Rutba Office

The words the Urdu catalogue (`ur.js`) uses for the suite's recurring terms,
so the same thing is called the same everywhere. It follows the words people
already know from Microsoft Office and Windows in Urdu where those exist, in
Pakistan's usage, and plain everyday Urdu where they do not.

**This translation was drafted by machine and needs a native speaker's
review** before it is called finished. A reviewer changes a word here first,
then in `ur.js`, so the two stay in step.

## How the catalogue is written

- **Commands** take the polite imperative, as Office does: محفوظ کریں (Save),
  کھولیں (Open), بند کریں (Close), داخل کریں (Insert).
- **Names of things** (a tab, a group, a pane) take the noun: ہوم، داخل کریں،
  ڈیزائن، لے آؤٹ، جائزہ، منظر.
- **Placeholders** such as `{name}` and `{count}` stay exactly as written, in
  braces, untranslated; Urdu word order moves them where Urdu wants them.
- **Keys and shortcuts** stay as they are: Ctrl+S, F7, Shift+F2.
- **File extensions, formats and codes** stay as they are: .docx, PDF, CSV,
  `=SUM(ABOVE)`, `dd/mm/yyyy`.
- **Brand and product names** stay in Latin letters: Rutba Office, Word,
  Excel, PowerPoint, Office, Windows, Google, Microsoft, iCloud.
- **Punctuation**: the Urdu comma ، and question mark ؟; a full stop is ۔ at
  the end of a sentence; the ellipsis … stays at the end of a command that
  opens a dialog, as in English.
- **Counts** use Urdu's two plural forms, `one` and `other`:
  `{ one: '{count} سلائیڈ', other: '{count} سلائیڈز' }`.
- **Numbers** stay in the digits Pakistan uses (0–9); the window formats them.

## Errors and plain sentences

Error messages and tooltips are full sentences: translate the meaning, in
plain polite Urdu, not word for word. Keep a sentence's facts (a number, a
name in `{braces}`, a key) exactly. Prefer "یہ فائل نہیں کھل سکی" over a
literal rendering.
